One point, three frames: NED, body, camera

C

Why "down is positive z", and why 5 m up is z = −5

The reason

Aircraft use a right-handed frame with x forward and y right. The right-hand rule then puts z down. NED follows the same pattern on the ground: North, East, Down.

With z down, a positive z is below the origin. A drone 5 m above home is 5 m in the negative z direction. So its z is −5.

Same idea, other places

GLOBAL_POSITION_INT.vz is positive down. A climb gives a negative vz.

GLOBAL_POSITION_INT.relative_alt is the exception. It is height above home, positive up, in millimetres.

The common bug

A script asks for z = +5 and expects a climb. In NED, z = +5 is 5 m below home. Always say which frame a number is in, and check the sign.

D

The rotation idea (concept, not code)

Three angles describe the attitude. Yaw ψ turns the nose around the down axis. Pitch θ lifts the nose. Roll φ lowers the right side. This page applies them in the order yaw, then pitch, then roll. This order is common in aerospace.

The three turns combine into one rotation matrix R. R takes a vector written in body axes and gives the same vector in NED axes. Its transpose does the opposite.

R = Rz(ψ) · Ry(θ) · Rx(φ)
vNED = R · vbody     vbody = RT · vNED
positive ψ: nose right · positive θ: nose up · positive φ: right side down

The camera frame has a fixed relation to the body frame. That relation comes from the camera mount. The camera also sits at an offset from the center of the drone. ArduPilot has parameters for these mount details, for example PLND_YAW_ALIGN and PLND_CAM_POS_X/Y/Z.

Two different things can change a number: a turn of the frame and a move of the origin. Keep them apart. A turn changes the direction of the axes. A move changes the point that you measure from.

Your M2 and M3 work. You write the chain from camera to body to NED yourself. This page gives you a picture to test your numbers against. It does not give you the conversion code.

E

Check yourself

Work out each answer first. Then use the scene to test it.

1. The drone is level, nose North, 5 m above home. The pad is 3 m North of home. What is the pad in body FRD?
About (3, 0, 5). The pad is 3 m ahead, 0 m to the side and 5 m below. Set yaw 0, height 5, pad N 3, pad E 0 to check.
2. Same scene, but the nose points East (yaw 90°). What is the pad in body FRD now?
About (0, −3, 5). North is now on the left side of the drone, and left is negative y in FRD.
3. Same as question 1. Where does the pad appear in the camera image?
Above the image center, on the vertical center line. In this mount the top of the image points to the nose. The camera y value is negative, because camera y points down in the image.
4. The drone pitches nose down 20°. The pad does not move. Does the pad move in the camera image? Why does this matter for M3?
Yes. The camera turns with the body, so the pad moves in the image. A detector that ignores attitude reads a turn as a move of the pad. ArduPilot's precision landing expects a frame that it can correct with attitude, for example MAV_FRAME_BODY_FRD.
5. The drone climbs from 2 m to 7 m. What are the start and end NED z values? What is the sign of vz during the climb?
z goes from −2 to −7. vz is negative during the climb.
