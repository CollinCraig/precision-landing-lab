How a drone flies (and who flies it)

The autopilot flies the drone. Your script sends requests to the autopilot and watches the result. This page shows the parts, the flight modes, arming, and the MAVLink messages between them.

A

The autopilot and your script

Two jobs, two speeds

ArduPilot keeps the aircraft stable. It reads the sensors and changes the motor speeds many times each second.

Your script works at a higher level. It asks for a mode, for arming, or for a height. Then it reads the state that ArduPilot reports.

Think of air traffic control

A controller gives instructions to a pilot. The pilot can refuse an instruction. The controller then looks at the radar to see what the aircraft does.

SITL

In this lab, the motors and sensors are a physics model on your laptop. ArduPilot runs its real code. Your script sees no difference.

B

Flight modes: who is in charge

A flight mode tells ArduPilot which rules to use. The mode decides who gives the targets: a human pilot, a script, or a stored mission. ArduPilot reports the current mode in every HEARTBEAT, in the custom_mode field.

A mode change is also a request. ArduPilot can refuse it. For example, a mode that needs a position estimate can refuse when the estimate is not good.

C

Arming and pre-arm checks

What arming means

"Armed" means that the motors can turn. "Disarmed" means that the motors stay stopped. The armed flag is bit 128 (MAV_MODE_FLAG_SAFETY_ARMED) of base_mode in each HEARTBEAT.

Pre-arm checks

Before ArduPilot arms, it runs pre-arm checks. If one check fails, ArduPilot refuses to arm. Examples from the ArduPilot list: "Compass not calibrated", "Gyros not healthy", "Battery below minimum arming voltage".

ArduPilot sends the failure reason as a text message to the ground station. While the vehicle stays disarmed, it repeats failing checks about every 30 seconds.

In SITL

Right after start, the position estimate is not ready yet. So arming fails for some seconds. This is normal. Wait for the checks to pass.

After arming

Arming does not make the drone fly. In GUIDED mode, your script sends a takeoff request after arming.

ArduPilot can disarm again by itself. For example, the Copter docs say that the motors disarm if the throttle stays at minimum for 15 seconds. After landing, DISARM_DELAY sets the delay before the automatic disarm.

Lesson

"Armed" is a state that can change at any time. Read it from the latest HEARTBEAT. Do not remember it from your last command.

Where the reasons appear

The reason text arrives in a STATUSTEXT message. The text often starts with "PreArm:". UNVERIFIED

D

MAVLink is a stream of small messages

Message meaning

Push Play. The scene connects, waits for pre-arm, sets GUIDED and arms. Each packet appears in the log when it arrives.

Rates in this animation

The rates here are slow so that you can follow them. ILLUSTRATIVE On RF links, systems usually send HEARTBEAT at 1 Hz. A system that misses four or five heartbeats is usually treated as lost.

MAVLink has no "function call". Each side sends small typed packets. A packet goes out, and the sender gets no automatic result. The receiver can answer with another packet, but a packet can also get lost.

Things to try

1. Push "Arm" right after a restart. Read the reason in the log.

2. Use "Difficult". Arm several times. Sometimes the ACK says ACCEPTED, but the state stays DISARMED.

3. Arm, then wait. The toy disarms on the ground after 10 seconds without a takeoff. ILLUSTRATIVE

4. Take off. Look at the height values near the end. The value comes near 5 m, but it is not exactly 5.000.

The rule

A command is a request. The ACK tells you that ArduPilot received the request and its first answer. The state tells you what happened. Confirm from the state.

The MAVLink command docs say this directly: "accepted" means that the flight stack will attempt the action. It does not mean that the action is complete.

Toy only

The toy picks its result codes and timings for teaching. Real ArduPilot result codes for each case can be different. ILLUSTRATIVE

F

Check yourself

1. Your script sends "arm" and gets no ACK. What do you know about the armed state?
You know nothing new. The request or the ACK can be lost. Read the armed flag from the next HEARTBEAT. The MAVLink command protocol says that a sender re-sends a command when no ACK arrives.
2. The ACK says ACCEPTED for a takeoff. Two seconds later, relative_alt is still 0. Is the takeoff done?
No. ACCEPTED is only a first answer. The height value is the evidence. Think about how long you wait, and what your script reports if the height does not change.
3. Which message tells you the flight mode, and which field?
HEARTBEAT, field custom_mode. For ArduCopter, GUIDED is 4 and LAND is 9.
4. Why does arming fail for some seconds after SITL starts?
The pre-arm checks need a healthy position estimate. The estimate needs some seconds to become ready. ArduPilot sends the reason as text.
5. Why do you compare height against a threshold, not an exact value?
Real and simulated values have small errors and small oscillations. A reading of 4.93 m is a normal "5 m". An exact compare can wait forever.
