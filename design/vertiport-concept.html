Rooftop vertiport, scaled to a 1 m pad

A small rooftop vertiport laid out with FAA EB 105 proportions and EASA marking conventions, sized for a small quadcopter. The AprilTag sits inside the touchdown ring, where a pilot's aim point would be.

Why the tag goes here

A real pad already has an aim mark: the touchdown ring. Placing the tag inside it gives the vertiport a machine-readable aim point without adding anything that looks out of place. The “V” stays in the FATO on the approach side, so the detector never sees it next to the tag.

B

Approach, descent and go-around profile

State machine (overlay names)

SEARCH
Loiter near the pad until the tag is valid for N frames.
APPROACH
Translate overhead and stay above the approach surface.
DESCEND
Vertical descent with ArduPilot PLND fed by LANDING_TARGET.
ABORT
Above the gate and the target is lost, confidence drops, or the pad is occupied: climb straight up, then hold or go around.
LANDED
Disarmed on the pad and stays down for 5 s. Pass if error ≤ 25 cm.

Real-world basis

EB 105 requires an 8:1 approach surface and at least two paths, ideally reciprocal or ≥ 135° apart. EASA's obstacle-free volume rises straight up from the safety area, then flares out as a funnel. That vertical profile is what our drop-in descent uses.

C

M3 video overlay: downward camera HUD

HUD elements

Box
Detected tag quad, corner ticks, id, size and decision margin.
Markers
Ring = estimated pad centre, ✕ = truth, dashed line = estimate error.
Error
Live horizontal error vs truth in cm, plus est − truth and a 10 s sparkline against the 25 cm limit.
State
Five chips. ABORT and LANDED also show a banner.
Confidence
0–1 bar with the minimum threshold tick. It turns amber, then red, as it falls.
Inset
Top-down offset in NED: aircraft at centre, rings at 10 and 25 cm.

Data it needs

Everything comes from the run directory: the TargetEstimate stream (valid, confidence, position, latency), the mission state, simulator truth (evaluation only) and detector corners per frame. Rendering happens after the flight, so the HUD can never feed back into control.
